# GHOST Protocol Codex
## Procedural Intelligence Environments and Experimental Systems

**Research Domain:** Scientific Infrastructure / Experimental Environments  
**Parent Laboratory:** SciINFRA  
**Document Status:** Foundational Research Specification

---

## 1. Purpose

The GHOST Protocol Codex defines a formal research framework for procedural environments, constraint-based simulations, state machines, experimental systems, and benchmark development.

GHOST investigates how intelligent systems interact with environments governed by explicit rules, dynamic constraints, temporal relationships, spatial conditions, and synchronization requirements.

Its purpose is to provide structured environments in which complex behavior, adaptation, constraint satisfaction, and emergent coherence can be investigated.

## 2. Core Research Dimensions

GHOST is organized around five foundational research dimensions:

1. Temporal causality
2. Spatial equilibrium
3. Synchronization
4. Emergent coherence
5. Constraint-based state transitions

These dimensions provide the foundation for procedural environment construction and experimental evaluation.

## 3. Temporal Causality

Temporal causality concerns the relationships between events, state changes, and the sequence of operations within an environment.

Research includes:

- Event ordering
- Temporal dependencies
- Cause-and-effect relationships
- State progression
- Time-dependent constraints
- Delayed consequences
- Temporal synchronization
- Causal consistency

Experiments should establish whether agents correctly interpret event sequences and respect temporal dependencies.

## 4. Spatial Equilibrium

Spatial equilibrium investigates relationships between objects, agents, boundaries, resources, and environmental conditions.

Research includes:

- Spatial relationships
- Position and displacement
- Geometric constraints
- Environmental boundaries
- Resource distribution
- Equilibrium conditions
- Spatial conflicts
- Dynamic environmental adaptation

Spatial constraints may be static or dynamically modified during experimentation.

## 5. Synchronization

Synchronization examines the coordination of events, agents, processes, and state transitions.

Research includes:

- Multi-agent synchronization
- Event coordination
- Temporal alignment
- Distributed state consistency
- Dependency resolution
- Coordination failures
- Synchronization recovery

The objective is to investigate how complex systems maintain coordinated behavior under changing conditions.

## 6. Emergent Coherence

Emergent coherence concerns the formation of consistent system-level behavior from interacting components and constraints.

Research includes:

- Local and global consistency
- Distributed coordination
- Emergent organization
- Constraint satisfaction
- Stability
- Adaptive coherence
- Conflict resolution
- Collective behavior

The existence of coherence must be evaluated against explicit criteria and observable system behavior.

## 7. Procedural Environment Architecture

Each GHOST environment should define:

- Environment identifier
- Environment class
- Initial configuration
- State variables
- Environmental rules
- Constraints
- Transition conditions
- Agent interaction model
- Observation mechanisms
- Evaluation criteria
- Reset conditions
- Termination conditions

Environments should be modular, reproducible, and independently versioned.

## 8. State Machines and Transition Systems

GHOST uses explicit state representations to model environment behavior.

Research components include:

- Initial states
- Valid states
- Transition rules
- Conditional transitions
- Invalid states
- Terminal states
- State dependencies
- Recovery conditions

Transitions should be governed by defined conditions rather than undocumented behavioral assumptions.

## 9. Constraint and Guardian Systems

GHOST investigates formal constraint systems that govern permissible environmental states and transitions.

Guardian systems may include:

- Boundary guardians
- Temporal guardians
- Spatial guardians
- Synchronization guardians
- Coherence guardians
- State-integrity guardians

Guardian classifications identify the type of constraint being monitored or enforced.

They do not independently establish that an environment is secure or correctly implemented.

## 10. Agent Interaction

GHOST environments may support:

- Individual agent experiments
- Multi-agent experiments
- Agent-environment interaction
- Cooperative behavior
- Competitive behavior
- Adaptive strategies
- Emergent coordination
- Constraint adherence

Agent behavior should be recorded against the environment's formal state and transition history.

## 11. Experimental and Benchmark Systems

GHOST environments may serve as reusable benchmark scenarios.

Evaluation dimensions include:

- Temporal reasoning
- Spatial reasoning
- Synchronization accuracy
- Constraint satisfaction
- State-transition correctness
- Adaptation
- Environmental stability
- Emergent coherence
- Recovery from invalid states

Each benchmark should include a defined problem, initial conditions, expected constraints, measurement methodology, and reproducible results.

## 12. Experimental Reproducibility

Every GHOST experiment should document:

- Codex version
- Environment version
- Configuration
- Initial state
- Agent or model version
- Execution parameters
- Random seed where applicable
- Event history
- State transitions
- Observed outcomes
- Evaluation results

## 13. Integration with SciINFRA

GHOST may use SciINFRA's:

- Sandbox infrastructure
- Computational resources
- Agentic systems
- Dataset engineering
- Benchmark frameworks
- Evaluation systems
- Research artifact management

GHOST retains its own environment rules, state models, guardian taxonomy, and protocol specifications.

It is not interchangeable with the general SciINFRA sandbox architecture.

## 14. Research Development Lifecycle

1. Define an experimental problem.
2. Specify environmental constraints.
3. Define state variables and transition rules.
4. Construct the procedural environment.
5. Implement observation and instrumentation.
6. Validate environment behavior.
7. Execute controlled experiments.
8. Measure outcomes.
9. Document results and limitations.
10. Publish reproducible research artifacts.

## 15. Development Status

GHOST components are classified as:

- Proposed
- Specified
- In development
- Operational
- Validated

Formal specifications and implemented environments must be tracked separately.

## 16. Long-Term Objective

Develop GHOST into a reusable procedural intelligence environment and benchmark ecosystem capable of supporting research into temporal causality, spatial equilibrium, synchronization, emergent coherence, state transitions, and constrained intelligent behavior.
